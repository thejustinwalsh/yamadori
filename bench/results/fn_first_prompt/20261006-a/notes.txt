fn_first_prompt 20261006-a: why Flash-Next's first prompt after a load is slow (synthetic prompts only; no keys)

Setup: deployed flash-next (cand0029, LLAMA_PIN_EXPERTS=0, -b 8192 -ub 512, layer-major 3 slots) on the 5060 Ti; bonsai-a4000 and
embeddings loaded on the A4000 and left alone. ~24.6K-token fresh prompts (system of numbered guidelines in a seeded order with a random
header, plus the soak's first user task), max_tokens 24, streamed, sent to llama-swap :11434 model `flash-next` (NOT through the proxy
at :1234: no test account key was available to this run; the proxy adds heartbeats, not prefill work). Every prompt reports cached 0.
Unload: POST /api/models/unload/flash-next, confirmed absent from GET /running before the request. Counters: bench/fn_first_prompt_sampler.ps1
(Get-Counter, ~1 s). Per-batch timings: the server's own `prompt processing, n_tokens = N ... t = T s` lines from /logs/stream/flash-next.
summary.txt is generated by `python bench/fn_first_prompt.py summarize <dir>` from the raw files.

Files: requests*.jsonl (one row per request, with the server's `timings`, usage, mem snapshots, matched log lines), samples*.jsonl (counters),
serverlog*.jsonl (server lines with arrival times), stdout_*.txt. Tags: -c0 = one fresh prompt on the already-loaded server after ~4 h idle
(its sample timestamps were 18,000 s ahead -- Get-Counter's Timestamp -- and were corrected by -18000 s; later runs use the system clock);
_natural = unload then reload at once (file cache as the previous run left it); _cold = unload, then 66.4 GB of the unused Q2_0 shards read
through the cache (27.6-29.0 s each) so the IQ2_XS pages leave it, then reload; _preread = the same eviction, then IQ2_XS shard 1 (39.2 GB)
read front to back with 16 MiB cached reads (17.0-17.4 s, 2.25-2.31 GB/s), then reload. Shard 2 (26.8 GB, the n-gram table, read by rows) is
not pre-read: both shards together (63.4 GB) exceed what the cache can hold and would push shard 1 out.

Known limits: n=3 per arm (n=1 for -c0); the eviction is a stand-in for what other loads do to the cache in real use; a1 of _natural and
_preread are slower than their a2/a3 and the cause of the _preread one (0.9 GB disk, load 24.5 s) is not established.
